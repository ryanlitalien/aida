Where the time goes, per option

Five requests per option, same 49-character reply (“The nine o'clock meeting has moved to eight, sir.”), Jarvis's voice and settings. Each dot is one real request; the bar is the mean. The spread matters as much as the position — that is the whole case against Flash.

The finding that changes the payoff

The current model's time-to-first-audio scales with reply length; Flash and Turbo stay flat. A short reply costs it ~830ms, but a paragraph costs it two full seconds. Longer answers — briefings, aida_query results — are exactly where the delay is worst today, and exactly where the switch pays most.

Does it still sound like them?

The one question no measurement settles. The voice ID is unchanged in every clip — the model is the synthesis engine, not the voice asset — so the risk is not a different voice but a flatter one. ElevenLabs concedes emotional nuance is Flash and Turbo's weak point, and Aida's persona deliberately rides on it (stability 0.40, style 0.40, tuned for wit and sarcasm). Listen to Aida's three clips back to back; that pair is the actual decision. Jarvis, at stability 1.0 and style 0.05, has almost nothing to lose.

Caveats worth keeping

  • N=5 Five samples per option. Enough to separate 827ms from 309ms with confidence, and enough to expose Flash's variance, but not enough to call Turbo's 309ms meaningfully different from Flash-with-osl's 316ms. Those two are a tie on this evidence.
  • NETWORK Measured from one machine on one connection in a single session. Roughly 375ms of any TTFB here is TCP/TLS setup that no model choice removes — which is also why the floor sits near 250ms rather than the ~75ms vendor inference figure.
  • UNTESTED eleven_v3 was deliberately excluded: it bills at the same 1 credit/char as today and ElevenLabs states it is not built for real-time use, so it cannot win on either axis. Not tested, not ruled out on quality grounds — ruled out on cost and latency.
  • SCOPE output_format was left at the default. Dropping to a lower bitrate would shave more off TTFB but trades audio quality, which cuts against keeping the voices intact.
